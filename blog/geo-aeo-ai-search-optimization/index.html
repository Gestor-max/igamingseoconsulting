GEO and AEO: How to Get Your Brand Recommended by AI Search

Search is shifting from lists of links to AI-generated answers. Here's how Generative Engine Optimization and Answer Engine Optimization work — and how to prepare.

A growing share of your audience no longer clicks through ten blue links. They ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s AI Overviews — and act on the answer.

For brands, this changes the goal: it’s no longer enough to rank. You need to be the source AI engines cite.

GEO vs. AEO: what’s the difference?

AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) is about structuring content so search engines can extract a direct answer: clear headings, concise definitions, FAQ markup, tables that resolve a query in one glance.

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) goes further: optimising so generative models select, trust, and reproduce your content in their synthesized answers. It rewards entity clarity, authoritative sourcing, and content structured for extraction.

Think of AEO as getting the featured snippet. GEO as becoming the reference the AI quotes.

What AI engines reward

Analysis of how generative engines select sources shows consistent patterns:

  1. Extractable structure — self-contained answers under clear headings, statistics with citations, and definitions that make sense out of context.
  2. Entity clarity — consistent naming, structured data, and strong off-site corroboration so the model knows who you are and what you’re an authority on.
  3. Genuine expertise — unique data, first-hand experience, and original analysis. AI engines are trained to prefer primary sources over rehashed listicles.
  4. Citation-worthy stats — quantified claims in clean sentences get lifted into answers, with your brand attached.

Practical steps to start now

  • Add FAQ sections to your core pages with question-formatted headings (### How much does X cost?) and 40–60 word answers.
  • Publish original data — surveys, benchmarks, campaign results. Original statistics are the most-cited content type in AI answers.
  • Strengthen your entity footprint: consistent brand information, schema markup, and mentions in recognised publications.
  • Write conclusions that summarize cleanly. Generative engines lean heavily on closing statements.

Why this matters more in regulated verticals

In finance and iGaming, buyers ask AI assistants the same questions they’d hesitate to type into Google: “Which platform is safest?”, “What should I look for in an advisor?” Being the brand the AI recommends — with the trust signals to back it up — is the new front page.

The winners of the next five years won’t be the brands with the most content. They’ll be the ones AI engines trust enough to quote.
