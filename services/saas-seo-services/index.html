Why SaaS SEO is different

Traffic ≠ revenue

A blog post with 50k visits can produce zero signups. SaaS SEO is measured in trials, demos and pipeline — every keyword and page is mapped to a funnel stage.

The buyers are researchers

B2B buyers compare, validate and benchmark before they talk to sales. If you're absent from "alternative", "vs" and review SERPs, you're not in the deal.

Software SERPs are brutal

G2, Capterra, Gartner and listicles dominate commercial software keywords. Beating them needs authority, intent-matched pages and structured data — not generic content.

Speed compounds

Paid acquisition costs rise forever; organic compounds. A SaaS SEO asset built this quarter keeps producing signups while you sleep — if the foundation is right.

Frequently asked questions

How long does SaaS SEO take to produce signups?

Quick wins on bottom-funnel comparison keywords can land in 2–4 months. A durable organic pipeline usually takes 6–9 months of consistent execution. B2B SaaS cycles are longer — SEO must feed the funnel, not just rank.

Should our blog live on the same domain as our app?

Almost always yes. Consolidating blog, docs and marketing site on one domain concentrates authority and avoids subdomain crawling/rendering issues. I assess your specific setup during the audit.

Can you beat G2, Capterra and big listicles?

For "best X software" queries, directories are strong — but "alternative", "vs", pricing and integration keywords are winnable, and they're closer to the signup. Strategy targets where you can actually rank and convert.

How do you report SEO to a SaaS leadership team?

In pipeline terms: organic signups, demo requests, assisted revenue and cost-per-acquisition versus paid channels. Rankings and traffic are diagnostics — revenue is the metric.
